# Go10 voice and style

Read this fully before drafting or editing any post. The reference sample is `content/posts/welcome-to-go10.md`. Reread it when in doubt.

## Who is talking

Mateo: a software engineer who loves animation and is learning its pipeline side in public. He is a curious peer, not an expert lecturing. Readers are developers, animation students, and people with a software background thinking about entering the industry, and hiring people may read too. All of them should be able to just read the post and come away knowing something true.

## Tone

- First person, plain, conversational. "I", not "we" and not "one".
- Honest about the edge of his knowledge. "As far as I understand", "I haven't tried this yet", "studios do this differently" are good, used where they're true, not as a tic.
- Concrete over abstract. Name the tool, the department, the file format, the thing that goes wrong.
- Warm but not gushing. He likes this field; show it through specifics, not adjectives.
- Explain jargon the first time it appears, in half a sentence, the way you'd explain it to a smart friend from web development.

## Writing style

- Natural English, short to medium sentences. Mix lengths, but never to perform rhythm.
- Start with what the thing is or why it matters to someone making a film or show. Skip warm-up.
- Say the thing directly. If a sentence can lose a word without losing meaning, cut it.
- Lists only when the content is a real list (steps, options, who it's for). Most of a post is paragraphs.
- Headings are plain labels ("Who this is for"), sentence case, and only if the post needs navigation.
- End when the point is made. A closing line may invite the reader to follow along or point to something, like the welcome post does, but it doesn't summarize what was just said.
- Links to primary sources inline, where the claim is made.

## AI-writing patterns to avoid

These make text read as machine-written, and readers who spot them stop trusting the rest. Treat them as a checklist on every draft and every edit.

**Words and phrases.** Avoid: delve, dive into, deep dive, landscape, realm, tapestry, testament, ecosystem (unless literally about software ecosystems), leverage, harness, unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, streamline, seamless(ly), robust, crucial, pivotal, vital, key (as filler), game-changer, cutting-edge, revolutionize, transformative, navigate (abstractly), foster, underscore, showcase, intricate, nuanced, myriad, "a rich tapestry", "in today's fast-paced world", "it's important to note", "it's worth noting", "let's explore", "in conclusion", "ultimately", "at the end of the day".

**Sentence shapes.**
- "It's not just X, it's Y" and "Not only X but also Y" framings.
- "Not X. Not Y. Just Z." and other staged reveals.
- Rhetorical questions the author immediately answers ("The result? A faster pipeline.").
- Rule-of-three lists used by reflex ("fast, reliable, and scalable"). Use three only when there are three.
- Participial tails that add fake insight: ", highlighting the importance of...", ", reflecting a broader shift...", ", making it easier than ever to...".
- Opening with a sweeping claim about the industry or history before getting to the point.
- Closing paragraphs that restate the post, or end on an inspirational line.
- Short punchy fragments stacked for drama. One, occasionally, is fine.

**Formatting.**
- Em dashes. Use none. Rewrite with a period, comma, colon or parentheses.
- Bold run-in headers on every bullet ("**Speed:** ..."). Emoji. Headers on short sections. Tables for things that aren't tabular.
- Title Case Headings. Colons in titles used as "Topic: The Subtitle".

**Content.**
- Vague praise or vague authority ("many studios", "experts agree", "widely used") without a name or a source. Name the studio or drop the claim.
- Hedging every sentence, or no hedging anywhere. Hedge only what is actually uncertain.
- Balanced "on one hand / on the other hand" structure when Mateo has an opinion. Say the opinion.
- Fabricated experience, anecdotes, quotes or numbers. If it isn't in the notes, it isn't in the post.
- Analogies stretched past the point where they're accurate.

## Checklist before showing any draft

1. Search for em dashes (`—`) and en dashes used as dashes. There should be zero.
2. Search for every word and phrase in the lists above.
3. Read each paragraph's first and last sentence. Does the last one just restate the paragraph? Cut it.
4. Find every "not X, but Y" or "isn't just". Rewrite as a plain statement.
5. Find every list of three. Is it really three things?
6. Every factual claim has a source in the research notes. Every first-person claim of experience is something Mateo actually told you.
7. Every piece of jargon is explained once, near first use.
8. Read it aloud in your head. Would Mateo say this sentence to a friend over coffee? If it sounds like a press release, rewrite it.
